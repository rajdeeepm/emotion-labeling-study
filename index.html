RESEARCH LABELING TASK

Emotion Labeling Study

What you will do

Label the dominant emotion expressed in 5 tweets.

You will see five short messages selected from the Emotion dataset. For each message, choose exactly one of six emotion categories: anger, fear, joy, love, sadness, or surprise.

5tweets
6emotion choices
1label per tweet

Instructions

  1. Read the entire tweet. Tweets may contain informal grammar, abbreviations, or unusual wording.
  2. Identify the emotion expressed by the author. Label what the text communicates, not the emotion that the tweet causes you personally to feel.
  3. Choose exactly one label. Select the single emotion that best represents the tweet.
  4. If several emotions seem possible, choose the dominant one. Ask: “If I had to summarize the author’s emotional state with one of the six available labels, which is the closest fit?”
  5. Use only the text shown. Do not search for the original tweet, author, or surrounding context, and do not use an AI tool or another person to choose the label.
  6. There are no intentionally hidden extra categories. Even if a tweet feels ambiguous, choose the closest of the six categories.
  7. Complete all five tweets. Your assignment is saved, so entering the same participant ID again will resume the same five tweets rather than give you a new set.

The six labels

Use these definitions as your guide. Focus on the dominant emotional state expressed in the message.

Anger

Anger, irritation, frustration, resentment, hostility, or fury.

Fear

Fear, anxiety, worry, nervousness, apprehension, or feeling threatened.

Joy

Happiness, pleasure, excitement, contentment, delight, or positive enthusiasm.

Love

Affection, tenderness, fondness, care, attachment, warmth, or romantic feeling.

Sadness

Sadness, grief, disappointment, hopelessness, loneliness, sorrow, or feeling down.

Surprise

Astonishment, amazement, shock, wonder, or a reaction to something unexpected.
